### Added
- **Ebook content check at import** (#3015). When a download finishes, Bindery now compares the EPUB's own title and author with the book it was grabbed for. Only a definite mismatch counts, meaning the title differs (series suffixes such as `, Book 11` and subtitles are ignored) and the author is neither the book's author nor one of its aliases, so a pen name like Franklin W. Dixon for Leslie McFarlane still matches. A file with no title or author is never flagged. The new setting **Check ebook contents** (`import.ebook.content_check`, admin only, in Settings under General) is `off`, `warn` or `block` and defaults to `warn`: the file imports as before and the History row and webhook carry a `contentCheck` note saying what the file turned out to be. `block` is opt in and refuses the download the way a wrong language does, blocklisting the release and keeping the book Wanted. Match to book and manual import skip the check, and Retry import runs it again.
- **Audiobook content check at import** (#3246). The same idea for audiobooks, using the audio tags: Bindery reads the album tag of the downloaded files (the track title only when there is no album and at least two files share it) and flags a download that holds other books, such as the next volume or another title by the same author, which an author check could not see. Chapter and track titles, prologues, credits and promo tracks like excerpts and previews never count, and a download whose every file carries one other title (a translated edition) is not flagged. The new setting **Check audiobook contents** (`import.audiobook.content_check`, admin only) is `off`, `warn` or `block` and defaults to `warn`. In `warn` the audiobook imports and the History row and webhook list the other books found with their file counts. In `block` the download is blocked with a message such as "the files contain Corralled (9), Coming Home to Myself (8)", the release is blocklisted and the book stays Wanted. Match to book and manual import skip the check, Retry import runs it again, and at most 500 files are read per import.
- **Content check hardening** (#3015, #3246). A creator that names nobody (Unknown, Anonymous, a publisher, an email address) is read as no creator, a Russian, Japanese, Hebrew, Arabic or Chinese edition of a book catalogued in Latin letters is never flagged, and Dr/Mr/St, hyphen and apostrophe spellings and spelled numbers count as the same title. Audiobooks ignore placeholder, publisher, web address and narrator albums, do not flag fewer than 5 percent stray files, accept a subtitle alone as the book's title, no longer skip ordinary titles with words like complete or best of, stop after a 30 second budget, and record a note when a download passes only because every file carries one other title. The ebook note lists at most 10 files, and a release with more than 20 EPUBs is not checked at all.
- **Content check, second hardening** (#3015, #3246). A release with more than 20 EPUBs is no longer judged on its first 20, an author with one non-Latin alias can still disagree with a Latin creator, more real compilations (short stories, collected and complete works, `X Collection`, `The Best of <author>`) are skipped while ordinary titles are still checked, `Part Two` style subtitles are no longer taken for titles, surnames like House or Press are no longer read as publishers, and a bracketed email address or a dotted word like Dot.com no longer hides a real name or title.
